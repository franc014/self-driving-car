Trigonometry glossary

The vocabulary used in this workspace, defined for this project rather than for a textbook. Terms are listed roughly from most basic to most applied.

How to use this

When a lesson uses a word you cannot define in one sentence, come back here. The “in this project” line is the one that matters for reading car.js.

Angle
The amount of rotation between two directions. In this project, car.angle is measured in radians from straight up; a positive angle turns the car left. See the sign decoder.
Radian
The angle whose arc length equals the circle's radius. A full turn is 2π radians. JavaScript's Math.sin and Math.cos take radians, and the car's steering increment is 0.03 rad. See “Radians in one breath.”
Degree
A unit of angle equal to 1/360 of a full turn. 90° = π/2 radians. Degrees are convenient for humans; radians are what the math library wants. Convert with degrees * Math.PI / 180.
Sine (sin)
The ratio of the side opposite an angle to the hypotenuse. In the car's velocity triangle, sine is the sideways share of the speed: left = speed · sin θ. See the values table.
Cosine (cos)
The ratio of the side adjacent to an angle to the hypotenuse. In the car's triangle, cosine is the up share of the speed: up = speed · cos θ. See the values table.
Hypotenuse
The longest side of a right triangle, opposite the right angle. Here it is the velocity vector: its length is the speed, and its direction is the heading. See the velocity triangle.
Opposite
The side across from the angle you are measuring. Relative to the car's angle θ, the opposite side is the horizontal leg — the sideways movement. Sine uses this side.
Adjacent
The side that touches the angle you are measuring, other than the hypotenuse. Relative to the car's angle θ, the adjacent side is the vertical leg — the up movement. Cosine uses this side.
Unit circle
A circle of radius 1 centered on the origin. Every direction corresponds to one point on it. In this project, speed 1 gives the point (−sin θ, −cos θ), because the circle starts at up and the screen's y axis points down. See the unit-circle section.
Vector
A quantity with both magnitude and direction. The car's velocity is a vector; its position change (Δx, Δy) is a vector. A bare number like speed is a scalar.
Component
The part of a vector along one axis. The car's movement has an x component Δx and a y component Δy. Sine and cosine are the tools that produce them. See the decomposition.
Magnitude
The length of a vector, ignoring its direction. The magnitude of the car's velocity is its speed. If you know Δx and Δy, the distance moved is Math.hypot(Δx, Δy).
Heading
The direction the car's nose points. In this project, heading is stored as this.angle; the movement vector is always along the heading. See how to compute a heading from a target.
Coordinate system
The axes and sign conventions used to describe positions. On a canvas, the origin is top-left, x increases right, and y increases down. This is why the movement code subtracts both components. See the sign conventions.
Counterclockwise
Opposite to the direction clock hands move. On screen, a positive car angle turns counterclockwise — left. The left arrow adds to the angle.
Clockwise
In the direction clock hands move. Canvas's positive rotation is clockwise because its y axis points down. The car is drawn with rotate(−θ) to convert its counterclockwise angle into a canvas rotation. See the draw pattern.
Periodicity
The property of sine and cosine that repeats every 2π. The car's angle can grow past a full turn or go negative; the direction simply repeats, so the formulas keep working without normalization.
atan2
An inverse tangent that takes the sine and cosine separately, so it can distinguish all four quadrants. Use Math.atan2(-dx, -dy) to turn a screen-space direction back into the car's angle. See the inverse formula.
Speed
How fast the car moves along its heading, in pixels per frame. In this code, speed is signed: positive is forward, negative is reverse. It is not the same as velocity, which also includes direction.
Velocity
Speed together with a direction. The car stores speed and angle separately, then combines them into a velocity vector with sine and cosine. See the velocity triangle.
Displacement
The change in position over one frame: (Δx, Δy). The car adds a displacement to its position every frame; a path is the sum of many small displacements.

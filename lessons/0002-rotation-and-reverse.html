Lesson 02 · Drawing & reversing

The car that draws backwards

Two minus signs remain. One turns the drawing the right way; the other makes reverse steering feel like a car. Neither is a trigonometry trick — each belongs to a different system.

Time
15–18 minutes
Prerequisite
Lesson 01
Code under study
car.js, lines 46–65
Mission
derive, don't copy

Why this matters

The car already knows how to move. Now make the drawing agree with the movement, and make reverse steering do what a driver expects. By the end you will name all three minus signs in this project and say which system each one compensates for.

The movement formula uses the nose direction. The canvas draws in a different coordinate system. Reverse adds a third twist. Separate the three, and the code stops looking arbitrary.

Section 01The car is drawn at its own origin

Movement updates a point: this.x, this.y. Drawing does something else: it puts a local coordinate system at that point, rotates it, and draws a rectangle around the origin.

The rectangle is 30 × 50, drawn from (-15, -25) to (15, 25). The nose is at (0, −25), the rear at (0, +25). At angle 0, the nose points up and no rotation is needed. That matches Lesson 01: at angle 0, the car moves up.

Drawing never moves the car

update() changes this.x and this.y. draw() only translates and rotates the shape around that point. If the car looks like it is sliding sideways, the position is fine; the drawing and the heading disagree. That is a rotation-sign bug, not a movement bug.

Section 02Canvas rotation is clockwise

On a math plot, positive rotation runs counterclockwise. On a canvas, it runs clockwise, because the y axis points down. The rotation matrix is the same in both worlds; only the meaning of the result changes.

Canvas rotate(a) x′ = x cos a − y sin a
y′ = x sin a + y cos a

Feed the car's local nose (0, −1) into that matrix:

Where the nose points rotate(+a):   ( sin a, −cos a )   // up-right for a = +60°
rotate(−a):   (−sin a, −cos a )   // up-left for a = +60°
motion:        (−sin a, −cos a )   // the Lesson 01 heading

The comparison is the whole lesson. The movement vector is (−sin θ, −cos θ). The drawing matches it only when the rotation is negated. ctx.rotate(-this.angle) is therefore not a quirk: it is the exact bridge between the car's angle convention and the canvas's clockwise rotation.

Same angle, two coordinate systems

The car measures θ from the nose, with positive turning left. The canvas measures rotation from the local x axis, with positive turning clockwise. The minus sign is the conversion between those two conventions, just as the movement formula's minus signs are the conversion to screen axes.

Section 03Rotation lab: one angle, two drawings

Predict before you drag: at θ = +60°, which way does the correct car point? Which way does the wrong one point? Then move the slider and watch the motion arrow.

At 60°, the correct nose is (−0.87, −0.50): up and to the left, exactly the motion vector. The wrong nose is (+0.87, −0.50): up and to the right. The car would be drawn on the wrong side of its own path.

Section 04Reverse flips the velocity, not the formula

There is no separate reverse branch in #move(). The sign of this.speed does all the work. At angle 0, forward speed moves the car up; negative speed moves it down, even though the nose still points up.

The same formula, two signs of speed
θ speed Δx Δy Motion
0° +2 0 −2 Up, along the nose
0° −2 0 +2 Down, against the nose
90° +2 −2 0 Left, along the nose
90° −2 +2 0 Right, against the nose

A negative speed multiplies both components by −1. Geometrically, that reflects the velocity vector through the car's center: the arrow of motion points exactly opposite the nose. The car is still facing forward; it is simply traveling backward.

Reverse is a vector, not a mode

The code does not ask “am I in reverse?” before moving. It computes (−sin θ, −cos θ) · speed. A negative speed flips the vector. That is why reverse can reuse the entire movement system without a second formula.

Section 05Why steering flips in reverse

Now the second minus sign, the one in the steering block:

When you drive forward and press left, the nose turns left and the car curves left. That is the obvious case. In reverse, the car travels opposite its nose, so the same nose rotation sends the rear the other way. The driver is watching the rear, so the input would feel inverted.

flip fixes that. In reverse, pressing left turns the nose right, which sends the rear left — the direction the driver intended. The nose and the rear turn in opposite directions; the car's motion follows the rear.

One key, three different outcomes
Case Δ angle Nose turns Motion Rear goes
Forward + left +0.03 Left Forward-left Left
Reverse + left, no flip +0.03 Left Backward-right Right
Reverse + left, with flip −0.03 Right Backward-left Left

You can derive the first frame from Lesson 01. At θ = 0, one steering step changes the angle by ε. The sideways velocity is −sin ε · speed:

First-frame sideways motion forward, left:      θ = +ε, speed = +v → Δx = −sin ε · v < 0   (left)
reverse, no flip:   θ = +ε, speed = −v → Δx = +sin ε · v > 0   (right)
reverse, with flip: θ = −ε, speed = −v → Δx = −sin ε · v < 0   (left)

The steering input changes the heading. The speed sign changes the direction of travel. The flip changes the heading in the opposite direction so that the product — the rear's motion — follows the key.

Not a trigonometry fact

flip is a control convention. It models a front-wheel-steered car backing up. The movement formula is already correct without it; removing flip does not break the geometry. It breaks the feel: the rear would swing against the key in reverse.

Section 06Reverse lab: watch the rear

Set speed to Reverse and steering to Left. Predict where the nose points and where the motion arrow points. Then step the car and compare. Toggle the flip off and repeat.

With the flip on, pressing left in reverse sends the motion arrow left while the nose turns right. With the flip off, the nose turns left while the motion arrow goes right. The car is not steering backward; the rear is leading.

Section 07Three minus signs, three jobs

This project has three minus signs that look similar and mean completely different things. Naming the system each one belongs to is the skill.

Where each minus lives and what it compensates for
Minus Where Compensates for Remove it and…
x -=
y -=		 Movement Screen axes: left is −x, up is −y The car moves right and up when it should move left and down.
rotate(-θ) Drawing Canvas positive rotation is clockwise The car is drawn mirrored across its direction of travel.
flip = -1 Steering Reverse motion leads with the rear The rear swings against the key when reversing.

The one-sentence model

Movement converts the heading to screen axes; drawing converts the heading to canvas rotation; steering converts the key to the rear's direction of travel. Three conversions, three minus signs.

The next time a car points one way and moves another, do not change all three signs. Ask which system is disagreeing: the position, the drawing, or the steering.

Section 08Check your grip

Answer from memory. The feedback appears immediately; the explanation appears after you choose.

Five quick sign checks

No calculator needed.

Q1 Why does draw() call ctx.rotate(-this.angle)?

Canvas y points down, so positive rotation looks clockwise. The car's positive angle turns counterclockwise, so the drawing negates it to agree with the movement.

Q2 At angle 0 and speed −2, the car moves…

At 0°, Δx = 0 and Δy = +2. The nose still points up, but the negative speed reverses the velocity, so the car moves down.

Q3 In reverse, pressing left with flip sends the rear…

flip turns the nose right in reverse. The car travels opposite its nose, so the rear goes left — the direction of the key.

Q4 If you remove flip and reverse while pressing left, the rear goes…

Without the flip, the nose turns left. The car travels opposite its nose, so the rear swings right. The geometry is fine; the steering feel is inverted.

Q5 Which minus sign compensates for canvas rotation?

Canvas positive rotation is clockwise; the car's positive angle is counterclockwise. The drawing negates the angle. The movement signs handle the screen axes; the flip handles reverse motion.

Section 09Predict, then drive

Open index.html and use the arrow keys. Predict each result before you press the key.

  1. Hold ArrowUp, then ArrowLeft. Which way does the nose point? Which way does the path curve?
  2. Hold ArrowDown to reverse, then ArrowLeft. Which way does the rear swing? Watch the back of the car, not the nose.
  3. Stop, then set car.angle = Math.PI / 2; in the console. Hold ArrowUp. The car should move left and be drawn nose-left. If the drawing points right, the rotation sign is wrong.

For a controlled experiment, make a copy of car.js before editing. This project has no git history to undo a change for you.

  1. Change ctx.rotate(-this.angle) to ctx.rotate(this.angle). The path still goes left, but the car is drawn nose-right. The movement and the drawing disagree. Restore the minus.
  2. Change the flip line to const flip = 1;. Reverse, then press left. The rear now swings right. Restore the conditional.

Challenge

At speed −2 and angle 0, hold left for exactly one frame. Predict the sign of Δx before running. With the flip on, it is negative; with the flip off, it is positive. Explain both without looking at the table.

Teach it back

Without looking, name the three minus signs and the system each one belongs to. Then explain why the car's nose turns right while the rear goes left in a reverse-left turn. If you can do that, the drawing and steering systems are yours.

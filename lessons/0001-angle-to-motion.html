Lesson 01 · Trigonometry for the car

One angle, two directions

The car's entire movement system is two lines of code. Derive them once, and you can predict the car's path in your head — no running the program.

Time
12–15 minutes
Prerequisite
SOHCAHTOA
Code under study
car.js, lines 46–58
Mission
derive, don't copy

Why this matters

Your mission is to learn the math deeply enough to derive it. By the end of this lesson you will predict the car's next position from its angle and speed, and explain why both terms are negative. Steering, sensors, and the rest of the self-driving car are built on this one triangle.

Start with the two lines that move the car. Everything else in car.js exists to feed them an angle and a speed.

Section 01The whole movement system is two lines

Steering does one thing: it adds or subtracts 0.03 from the angle. Acceleration does one thing: it changes the speed. The rest is these two lines.

Try the simplest case. If angle = 0 and speed = 2, then sin(0) = 0 and cos(0) = 1. So x does not change, and y drops by 2. On a canvas, y grows downward, so the car moves up. That matches the rectangle in draw(), whose nose points up at angle 0.

Now try angle = Math.PI / 2 (90°) and speed = 2. Then sin = 1 and cos = 0. The car's x drops by 2 and y does not change: it moves left. So a positive angle turns the car left. That is the convention the rest of the code assumes.

The question to keep asking

Why does sine move x here, when school taught you that sine goes with y? And why are both terms subtracted? The answers are not arbitrary. They come from where the angle is measured and where the canvas puts its axes.

Section 02Put the speed on the hypotenuse

The car's velocity is a vector: it has a length (the speed) and a direction (the angle). To move on a grid, split that vector into two perpendicular components — how much it moves up, and how much it moves left.

Draw the velocity as the hypotenuse of a right triangle. The angle θ sits at the car, between the nose (up) and the heading. The leg next to θ is the vertical leg. The leg across from θ is the horizontal leg.

Now read the triangle with SOHCAHTOA, treating each ratio as a percentage of the hypotenuse:

  • Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse: cos θ = up / speed, so up = speed · cos θ.
  • Sine is opposite over hypotenuse: sin θ = left / speed, so left = speed · sin θ.

The hypotenuse is not the car's length. It is the velocity vector, drawn from the car's center. The car itself is just the origin of that vector.

Why sine is on x here

In school, θ is usually measured from the +x axis, so cosine goes with x and sine goes with y. The car measures θ from its nose — straight up. Rotate your mental protractor 90° counterclockwise, and the roles swap. The formula did not change. The reference direction did.

Section 03The screen subtracts both components

A canvas coordinate system is not the math plot you drew triangles on. Its origin is the top-left corner: x increases to the right, and y increases downward. The car's up is therefore −y, and a left turn is −x.

Substitute those screen directions into the triangle:

Screen displacement for one frame Δy = −speed · cos θ    (up is negative y)
Δx = −speed · sin θ    (left is negative x)

That is exactly the code. Writing this.x -= Math.sin(...) * speed is the same as adding −sin θ · speed. The subtraction is not a quirk of the programmer. It is the screen's coordinate system showing up in the formula.

The sign decoder
Quantity Positive direction What the code does
Screen x Right Left movement subtracts from x.
Screen y Down Up movement subtracts from y.
Car angle θ Left / counterclockwise The left arrow adds 0.03 rad.
Canvas rotation Clockwise The car is drawn with rotate(−θ).

Canvas rotation is clockwise for positive angles because y points down. See MDN's canvas transformations guide and the rotate() reference.

What is a trig fact and what is a screen fact?

In a math plot with y pointing up, the same triangle gives (−sin θ, +cos θ). The minus on x comes from turning left; the minus on y comes from the canvas. Do not memorize the two lines as one inseparable blob. Separate the triangle from the coordinate system and you can re-derive them anywhere.

You will meet the same reconciliation in draw(): ctx.rotate(-this.angle). Canvas's positive rotation is clockwise, while the car's positive angle is counterclockwise. The minus sign makes the drawing agree with the movement. We will take that apart in the next lesson.

Section 04The unit circle is the speed = 1 case

Set the speed to 1. The formulas collapse to Δx = −sin θ and Δy = −cos θ. Plot that point for every possible θ and you get a circle of radius 1: the unit circle. Sine and cosine are simply the coordinates of a point on that circle, after rotating the starting point from +x to up and flipping y for the screen.

The right-triangle picture only works for angles between 0° and 90°. The circle extends the same pattern to every angle, including negative angles and angles past a full turn. That is why the car can keep turning and the formulas never break. See Khan Academy's unit circle introduction or Better Explained's intuitive trigonometry for the full picture.

Cardinal directions at speed 2
θ sin θ cos θ Δx = −2 sin θ Δy = −2 cos θ Screen direction
0° 0 1 0 −2 Up
90° 1 0 −2 0 Left
180° 0 −1 0 +2 Down
270° −1 0 +2 0 Right
360° 0 1 0 −2 Up again

Speed is fixed at 2 px/frame for this table. The real car allows reverse speeds down to −1.5; a negative speed flips both components, which is exactly what reverse should do.

The minus signs are fixed; the results are not

Both formulas carry a minus sign, but that does not make both components negative at every angle. Δx is negative only while sin θ > 0 — the nose is on the left half of the circle. Δy is negative only while cos θ > 0 — the nose points upward. At 120° the car points down-left: Δx stays negative, but Δy becomes positive. At −30° the car points up-right: Δy stays negative, but Δx becomes positive. The roles never swap: sine always scales the sideways component, cosine always the forward component. At 210° the nose is only 30° from straight down, so the down component is the larger one.

Radians in one breath

Math.sin and Math.cos take radians, not degrees. The car's steering adds 0.03 rad per frame, which is about 1.72°. A full turn is 2π ≈ 6.28 rad. The angle is never reset, and it does not need to be: sine and cosine repeat every 2π. Convert with degrees * Math.PI / 180 when you want human units.

Numbers worth knowing

At 30°, sin = 0.5 and cos ≈ 0.866. At 45°, both are ≈ 0.707. At 60°, they swap: sin ≈ 0.866, cos = 0.5. These four values cover most of the mental arithmetic you will do while debugging.

Section 05Heading lab: turn the angle, watch the components

Before you drag anything, commit to a prediction. At θ = 30° and speed 2, which is larger: the sideways step or the up step? Then check the readout. Hint: cos 30° is larger than sin 30°.

At 30°, the up step is 2 × cos 30° ≈ 1.73 and the sideways step is 2 × sin 30° = 1.00. The car moves farther up than left. At 45°, the two are equal. At 60°, the sideways step wins. The triangle tells you which before you press a key.

Section 06Check your grip

Answer from memory, not by scrolling. Each option gives immediate feedback, and the explanation appears after you choose.

Five quick predictions

All questions use speed = 2 px/frame.

Q1 θ = 0°. What is (Δx, Δy)?

At 0°, sin 0 = 0 and cos 0 = 1. So Δx = 0 and Δy = −2. The car moves straight up, which is negative y on the canvas.

Q2 θ = 90°. What is (Δx, Δy)?

At 90°, sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0. The full speed goes sideways: Δx = −2 and Δy = 0. The car moves left.

Q3 θ = 180°. What is (Δx, Δy)?

At 180°, sin = 0 and cos = −1. The formula gives Δy = −2 × (−1) = +2. The car moves down, which is positive y.

Q4 θ = 45°. What is (Δx, Δy)?

sin 45° = cos 45° ≈ 0.707, so each component is 2 × 0.707 ≈ 1.41. Both are negative: the car moves up and left.

Q5 Why does a left turn produce a negative Δx?

Moving left means decreasing x, and canvas x increases to the right. The negative sign comes from the screen convention, not from sine.

Section 07Predict, then run it

Now test the model against the real car. Open index.html in your browser. You can double-click the file; no server is needed. Open the developer console (in Chrome: Cmd + Option + J on macOS, or right-click the page and choose Inspect).

Before you press an arrow key, write down where the car will go for each of these headings. Then set the angle in the console and hold ArrowUp for a second.

  1. car.angle = 0; — predict first, then run.
  2. car.angle = Math.PI / 2; — predict first, then run.
  3. car.angle = Math.PI; — predict first, then run.
  4. car.angle = -Math.PI / 2; — predict first, then run.

If the car goes the wrong way, do not change the code yet. Check two things: the sign of y (up is negative) and the sign of the angle (positive is left). Those two conventions explain almost every "wrong direction" bug in this project.

Challenge

Set car.angle = Math.PI / 6 (30°). Without running it, decide whether the car will travel farther horizontally or vertically after one second of acceleration. Then verify. The ratio is fixed by tan 30° ≈ 0.577, no matter how fast the car gets.

One more observation: the current car has no momentum or steering physics. When you change car.angle while it is moving, the velocity direction changes instantly, so the path can turn sharply. That is why the car curves when you hold an arrow key: each frame adds 0.03 to the angle, and the next frame uses the new angle. A later lesson can add smoother steering.

Teach it back

Close this page. On a blank sheet, draw the triangle, label the two legs, and write the two movement lines from memory. Then say out loud why both are negative. If you can do that without looking, the movement system is yours.
